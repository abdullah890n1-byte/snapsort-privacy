1What we access

Your screenshots, and only your screenshots

SnapSort asks for access to your device's photo library so it can find your screenshots and show them to you inside the app. It does not browse your full camera roll for any other purpose, and it does not copy your images anywhere — it reads them directly from the photo library each time, the same way your Photos app does.

If you grant "limited" access instead of full access, SnapSort only ever sees the specific images you selected.

2What we store

A small local database, on your device, about your device

To organize your screenshots, SnapSort keeps a local database (a single SQLite file inside the app's own storage) containing:

  • Recognized text — read from each screenshot using on-device text recognition (Google's ML Kit, running on your device — the image and the text stay there)
  • Category and extracted fields — e.g. a receipt's total and merchant, a travel document's confirmation number
  • Your organization choices — favorites, "sensitive" flags, projects, collections
  • A content fingerprint (a SHA-256 hash of each image's bytes) — used only to find exact duplicate screenshots on your device, never to identify or compare against anything elsewhere
This database never leaves your device. It isn't backed up to any cloud service by the operating system either — SnapSort explicitly opts out of Android's automatic app backup for exactly this reason.
3What SnapSort doesn't do

No servers, no uploads, no accounts

SnapSort does not operate any server and has no account system. The app contains no code that uploads your screenshots, the text read from them, or your organization data to us or to any third party. There are no advertising SDKs and no analytics or crash-reporting service of our own.

Quick actions such as "Open in Maps" or "Track Flight" hand a link to your browser or Maps app, and only when you tap them.

4Diagnostics from Google ML Kit

The one thing that can leave your device

SnapSort uses Google's ML Kit for on-device text recognition and QR/barcode detection. According to Google's published data disclosure, ML Kit may send limited technical diagnostics to Google, encrypted in transit:

  • Device information — manufacturer, model, operating system version
  • App information — package name and version
  • Per-installation identifiers, which Google states are not intended to identify you personally
  • Performance metrics (such as processing time), configuration details, event types, and error codes

Google states that images and recognized text are never sent, and that ML Kit does not transfer this data to third parties. SnapSort does not receive or have access to these diagnostics. This is the reason the app's network permissions (section 7) exist. Google handles this data under its own privacy policy; its disclosure is published at developers.google.com/ml-kit/android-data-disclosure.

5Optional cloud processing

A setting that exists for the future, and is off today

AI Settings includes a "Cloud Processing" toggle, reserved for a possible future feature that would send select content to a cloud AI provider for deeper analysis. As of this version of the app, that toggle has no server to connect to — turning it on changes nothing yet. If this feature is ever activated in a future update:

  • It will be off by default and require your explicit opt-in
  • Anything you've marked "sensitive" will always be excluded, regardless of this setting — that exclusion cannot be bypassed
  • This policy will be updated first, with the specific provider and data involved named plainly
6The on-device assistant

A memory assistant, not a chatbot

The Memory Assistant answers questions about your saved screenshots using rules-based matching against your own local data — it is not a general-purpose AI model and does not send your questions or your data anywhere to generate an answer. If it can't find something in what you've actually saved, it says so rather than guessing.

7Permissions

What we ask for, and why

  • Photos / media (READ_MEDIA_IMAGES) — required to find and display your screenshots
  • Notifications (POST_NOTIFICATIONS) — only used if you turn on optional reminders in Profile › Notifications; off by default
  • Network access (INTERNET, ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE) — declared by Google's ML Kit library so it can send the diagnostics described in section 4. SnapSort itself makes no network requests of its own

SnapSort requests no location, contacts, microphone, camera, or background-location permissions.

8Deleting your data

Deletion is immediate, undo is generous

Deleting a screenshot inside SnapSort removes it from the app's organization (a short "Undo" window follows every delete). Because SnapSort never copies your images out of your photo library, removing something from SnapSort's database never deletes the original photo — you manage that from your device's Photos app, as always.

Profile › Backup & Sync lets you export a copy of your organization data to a file you control, and restore it later on the same device. Uninstalling SnapSort deletes its local database entirely, on-device, immediately — there is no SnapSort server copy to separately request deletion of, because none exists.

9Children's privacy

Not directed at children

SnapSort is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. SnapSort has no servers and no accounts; the only data that can leave the device is the technical diagnostics described in section 4, which contain no personal content.

10Your choices

You're already in control

Because there's no account and no SnapSort-held copy of your data, the usual "request my data" or "delete my account" process doesn't apply here in the way it does for cloud services — what the access-and-deletion rights under GDPR, CCPA, and similar laws are meant to guarantee is already true by default: your content lives only on your device, under controls you already have. For the ML Kit diagnostics in section 4, Google's own privacy policy and disclosure apply.
